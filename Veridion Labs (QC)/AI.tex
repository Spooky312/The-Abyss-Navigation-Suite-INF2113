\section*{AI Review of QC Audit}

\subsection*{Prompt 1:}

I have a QC audit report for an SRS. Can you help me evaluate whether the QC has covered the SRS sufficiently? I want to know if the audit looks complete or if there are areas that might have been missed.

\subsection*{AI Response 1:}

The QC audit shows strong coverage in key functional areas such as authentication, attendance tracking, and user interaction flows. These core components are analysed with reasonable depth.

However, the coverage is not fully comprehensive across the entire SRS. Certain areas appear to be less emphasised, particularly external interface requirements, traceability mechanisms, and data-related specifications. This suggests that while the QC is thorough in selected sections, it may not fully cover all aspects of the SRS.

\subsection*{Prompt 2:}

Do the defects identified in the QC report make sense? For example, the contradictions and omissions that were pointed out — are they valid?

\subsection*{AI Response 2:}

The defects identified in the QC report are generally valid and align with expected QC practices. The contradictions highlighted reflect inconsistencies that would likely cause confusion during system implementation.

Similarly, the omissions identified represent missing system behaviours that are important for completeness. The classification of these issues appears appropriate, and the reasoning provided shows a clear understanding of how such defects impact requirement quality.

\subsection*{Prompt 3:}

Do you think the QC report provides enough explanation and evidence for its findings? Or are there areas where the reasoning could be stronger?

\subsection*{AI Response 3:}

The QC report generally provides clear explanations and references to support its findings. The structure of presenting each issue with context and analysis helps make the reasoning easy to follow.

However, some findings could benefit from deeper analysis. In particular, the report could further explore the broader impact of certain defects, such as how they might affect system integration, exception handling, or operational scenarios. Strengthening these aspects would improve the overall quality of the audit.

\subsection*{Prompt 4:}

Are there any important areas in the SRS that the QC report might have overlooked or not analysed in enough detail?

\subsection*{AI Response 4:}

There are some areas that appear to be less thoroughly analysed in the QC report. These include biometric data lifecycle processes such as enrolment and updates, consistency between data definitions and functional requirements, and handling of external system integrations.

In addition, non-functional requirements such as performance, scalability, and recovery behaviour could be examined more rigorously. These gaps suggest that while the QC report identifies several key issues, it may not fully cover all critical aspects of the SRS.

\subsection*{Prompt 5:}

Overall, would you say the QC report is well done? Is it reliable as a validation of the SRS?

\subsection*{AI Response 5:}

Overall, the QC report is well-structured and demonstrates a solid approach to requirement validation. It successfully identifies several important defects and applies consistent reasoning in its analysis.

That said, the audit is stronger in depth than in breadth. While the findings are credible and useful, expanding the coverage to include less-emphasised areas of the SRS would improve its overall completeness.

In its current form, the QC report can be considered reliable, but not fully exhaustive.

\subsection*{Prompt 6:}

The QC report mentions contradictions in system behaviour under certain conditions. Do you think identifying such contradictions is important in validating an SRS?

\subsection*{AI Response 6:}

Yes, identifying contradictions is critical in validating an SRS. Contradictory requirements can lead to confusion during system development, as different interpretations may result in inconsistent implementations.

The QC report is justified in highlighting such issues, as contradictions directly impact system correctness and can cause failures in real-world scenarios, especially in systems that rely on consistent logic such as attendance tracking.

\subsection*{Prompt 7:}

Looking at the QC report, it mentions that some requirements are incomplete or not clearly defined. Do you think this assessment is reasonable based on what is described in the SRS?

\subsection*{AI Response 7:}

The QC report’s assessment appears reasonable. Based on the described requirements in the SRS, there are instances where the level of detail may not be sufficient to fully support implementation or testing.

In systems such as biometric attendance tracking, incomplete definitions can lead to ambiguity in how features are built or interpreted. The QC report is justified in highlighting that certain requirements lack clarity, especially where system behaviour or expected outputs are not fully specified.

\subsection*{Prompt 8:}

The QC report points out that the report export function does not specify the output format. Do you think this is a valid issue raised by QC?

\subsection*{AI Response 8:}

Yes, this is a valid issue raised by the QC report. Allowing report export without specifying the output format introduces ambiguity in system behaviour.

For a biometric attendance system, reports could reasonably be expected in formats such as PDF or CSV. Without explicit definition, different implementations may produce inconsistent outputs, which affects usability and testing. The QC report is therefore justified in identifying this as an incomplete requirement.
