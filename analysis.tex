\section*{Document Organization}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Document Organization}

This comprehensive report documents Brigman Deep Sea Technology's systematic application of the BCIC (Breakdown, Clarify, Interpret, Categorize) methodology to analyze Triton Deepwater's Project Specification for the Abyss Navigator Suite. Our focus remains exclusively on developing and implementing the software layer that will unify and coordinate all customer-specified hardware components.

Our project scope is clearly delineated: hardware components, specifications, and interfaces are exclusively specified by Triton Deepwater, while our software system serves as the critical integration layer that connects, coordinates, and manages all specified hardware components.

The analysis follows a structured methodology with hierarchical numbering for requirement traceability, standardized formatting, BCIC-xxx references for analysis artifacts, and a color-coded priority system (Critical, High, Medium). All analyzed software requirements comply with CRaM principles (Correct, Relevant, Measurable).

\section{Executive Summary}
\label{sec:executive}

Brigman Deep Sea Technology has successfully completed the comprehensive software requirements analysis phase for the Abyss Navigator Suite project using our proprietary BCIC methodology. Through systematic investigation and structured stakeholder engagement with Triton Deepwater, we have transformed ambiguous software requirements into measurable, testable specifications ready for implementation.

Our analysis of 27 formal Project Specification requirement clauses revealed 15 incongruities requiring clarification, representing a 55.6\% incongruity rate. Through targeted stakeholder sessions, we achieved an 86.7\% resolution rate, obtaining critical quantitative specifications including 99\% waypoint accuracy requirements, 5-second emergency detection thresholds, and AES-256 encryption standards.

The regulated-revision process demonstrated through our Interpret activity transformed ambiguous statements into codified requirements with clear verification methods. All requirements have been systematically categorized into functional domains including Navigation, Communication, Safety, and Interface specifications, preparing them for structured SRS development.

The value delivered through this early analysis is substantial, preventing an estimated \$350K+ in potential rework costs and reducing implementation risk by 4-6 weeks.

\subsection{Breakdown Activity: Systematic Incongruity Detection}

The Breakdown activity applies our Incongruous Requirements Checklist (IRC) to every requirement clause in the Project Specification. Each clause is examined through eight specific checks covering Imperative (obligation strength), Continuance (complexity), and Contextual (meaning, testability, consistency) dimensions. This process identified **15 incongruities** across the 27 clauses—a 55.6\% incongruity rate.

To illustrate the method, we present here the analysis of **BO-L1-4 (Emergency and Abnormal Situation Handling)**, the requirement that triggered the most IRC flags (three) and thus served as our primary example throughout stakeholder discussions. Table~\ref{tab:breakdown-example} shows the IRC application format for this requirement, including the specific clarification question prepared for the client.

During the clarification session with the Triton Deepwater Operations Team, the Account Manager presented these sketches on a large whiteboard. Stakeholders immediately engaged: they drew boundary lines, added missing actors (e.g., “Regulatory Authority”), and scribbled operational notes. For our example requirement **BO-L1-4**, the discussion centred on the “Emergency Handling” use case. Using the sketch, the client clarified:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Pressure deviation of \(\pm5\%\) sustained for 5 seconds constitutes an “abnormal condition”.
    \item Temperature deviation of \(\pm10\%\) from nominal, and power supply deviation of \(\pm15\%\), are also abnormal thresholds.
    \item Upon detection, the system must automatically transition the submersible to a **safe operational state**, defined as neutral buoyancy, minimal power consumption, and active communication beacon.
\end{itemize}

These clarifications, obtained through collaborative visual modelling, directly resolved the CTX‑1, CTX‑2, and CON‑2 flags for BO-L1-4. The same interactive process was repeated for all other flagged requirements; the paper napkin sketches evolved in real time into a shared understanding of system boundaries and operator interactions. Stakeholder feedback confirmed a **40\% reduction in clarification time** compared to text-only reviews.

\subsection{Interpret Activity: Regulated-Revision Demonstration}

Following the clarification session, we transformed the raw stakeholder inputs into precise, measurable, and verifiable software requirements. This **regulated-revision** process applies a fixed set of transformations:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Imperative issues (IMP):} replace weak or passive phrasing with strong obligations (“shall”).
    \item \textbf{Continuance issues (CON):} decompose compound statements into atomic requirements.
    \item \textbf{Contextual issues (CTX):} insert quantitative thresholds, define ambiguous terms, and add verification methods.
\end{itemize}

\noindent
For our example requirement, the transformation is shown in Table~\ref{tab:interpret-example}.

\subsection{Categorize Activity: Structured Organization for SRS Development}

With all requirements clarified and rewritten in measurable form, we performed systematic categorization following IEEE 830 guidelines. The refined requirements were grouped into four primary functional domains:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Navigation} – autonomous waypoint following, obstacle avoidance.
    \item \textbf{Communication} – telemetry, remote intervention, degraded mode handling.
    \item \textbf{Safety} – emergency detection, safe state management, fault recovery.
    \item \textbf{Interface} – sensor integration, API specifications, data logging.
\end{itemize}

Our example requirement (BO-L1-4) and its decomposed children (SAF-EMG-001…003, SAF-STATE-001) were assigned to the **Safety** domain. Each categorized requirement received a unique codification (e.g., SAF-EMG-001) that maintains full traceability to the original PS clause and to the corresponding IRC record.

This categorization was applied uniformly to all 27 original clauses, yielding **22 atomic requirements** ready for SRS development (81.5\% of the original set). The structured organization directly informs our repository architecture and enables incremental delivery planning. The complete categorised requirements list is maintained under version control at the repository cited in Section~7.

\section{Risk Analysis \& Mitigation}
\label{sec:risks}

Our comprehensive risk assessment identifies and addresses potential challenges that could impact project success, budget, or schedule.

\subsection{Critical Technical Risks: Software Integration Focus}

\textbf{Communication Specification Ambiguity} presented a high-severity risk due to missing quantitative bandwidth and latency specifications in the Project Specification. This risk corresponds to CTX-1 (Ambiguous term) and CTX-2 (Not testable) issues identified during Breakdown. With 72\% probability based on historical data, this ambiguity could lead to system failure under operational conditions. Our mitigation involved establishing specific requirements of 1 kbps minimum bandwidth with 30-second maximum latency.

\textbf{Hardware Interface Compatibility Issues} emerge from potential incompatibilities between customer-specified hardware components. This risk relates to CTX-1 issues regarding missing interface specifications. This medium-high severity risk carries 55\% probability given hardware specification diversity. Our mitigation strategy implements standardized middleware interfaces and adapter patterns.

\textbf{Sensor Integration Complexity} involves potential delays from missing interface specifications for hardware sensors, corresponding to CTX-1 and CON-1 issues. With 85\% probability based on specification reviews, this high-severity risk could impact project schedule. We mitigated through proposing and gaining approval for ROS2 interface standards.

\subsection{Project Management Risks: Delivery Focus}

\textbf{Scope Creep Post-Clarification} addresses potential introduction of new requirements during clarification sessions that could impact budget and schedule. This risk relates to CTX-3 (Inconsistent/out of scope) concerns about scope boundary maintenance. Our formal change control process with requirement traceability ensures all new requirements undergo impact analysis before acceptance.

\textbf{Hardware Specification Changes} considers potential modifications to customer hardware specifications during software development, which could trigger CTX-3 issues if not properly managed. We address this through contractual hardware specification freeze dates and formal change request processes.
